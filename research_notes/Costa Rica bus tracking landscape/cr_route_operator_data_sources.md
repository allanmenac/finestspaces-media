# Costa Rica bus routes and operators: data sources for building a GTFS feed (GAM focus)

Research date: 2026-10-10. Method note: the egress proxy blocked direct fetches of almost every target host (ctp.go.cr, aresep.go.cr, datos.go.cr, snitcr.go.cr, wiki.openstreetmap.org, overpass-api.de, transit.land, mobilitydatabase.org, feeds.simovi.org, delfino.cr, etc.). Findings below come from web-search result summaries (which quote the pages) and from the GitHub repository search. **No URL in this file was opened live from this environment, and no Overpass query was run.** Live checks are still needed: open each URL, run the Overpass query, and download the ARESEP endpoints. GitHub search and raw.githubusercontent.com did work.

## 1. CTP (Consejo de Transporte Público): registry of routes, concession holders, fleets, schedules and route paths

### Takeaway
Since mid-2026 the CTP has a public **Visor de Transporte Público**. It shows the authorized route paths (recorridos), branches (ramales) and stops for regular routes nationwide, and results can be exported in "several digital formats". It is the closest thing to an official geometry and stops registry. The CTP also has a "Datos Abiertos" section and a concession/permit-holder certificate lookup. No machine-readable CTP list of routes, operators, fleets and schedules was confirmed. The CTP's own data quality is criticized by the Contraloría and the Defensoría.

### Cited Findings
- The CTP launched a free interactive viewer of bus routes and stops for the whole country. Stops can be filtered by province, canton and district. It shows each route's ramales by direction of travel with their assigned stops, and lets you look up which routes use a given stop. Results can be downloaded in several digital formats. CTP staff used it internally from 2021, with interface improvements in 2025 and 2026. Article dated around 4 June 2026. — [Semanario Universidad](https://semanariouniversidad.com/pais/ctp-habilita-herramienta-para-que-ciudadania-consulte-rutas-y-paradas-de-autobus-en-todo-el-pais/); also [La Nación](https://www.nacion.com/el-pais/ahora-puede-consultar-en-linea-todas-las-rutas-y/HQFXAMB3YVAX3E3ZBU2PSR4VKY/story/), [Repretel](https://www.repretel.com/noticia/lanzan-plataforma-digital-para-consultar-rutas-y-paradas-de-autobus-en-todo-el-pais/)
- Viewer URL: [ctp.go.cr › Visor de Transporte Público](https://www.ctp.go.cr/servicios/plataforma-digital/visor-de-transporte-publico) (it has video tutorials). The CTP says the viewer is for reference only. Data may change after technical reports or board agreements, and certified information has to be requested through official channels. — [Repretel](https://www.repretel.com/noticia/lanzan-plataforma-digital-para-consultar-rutas-y-paradas-de-autobus-en-todo-el-pais/)
- The CTP homepage has a "Datos Abiertos" section that links to a CTP open-data portal. Its contents could not be confirmed. — [CTP](https://www.ctp.go.cr/)
- A Defensoría report used **CTP open data from October 2023**. It found only 22 concession routes with a signed and endorsed (refrendado) contract, and 266 pending. This shows the CTP has published at least some route and concession data. — [Semanario Universidad](https://semanariouniversidad.com/pais/defensoria-mas-de-un-centenar-de-rutas-de-bus-operan-sin-contrato-ni-refrendo/)
- The CTP offers a "Constancia Digital de Concesionario o Permisionario", a lookup by ID number for a single operator, not a bulk list. — [CTP](https://www.ctp.go.cr/servicios/plataforma-digital/constancia-de-concesionario)
- Regular-route service description page: [CTP Ruta Regular](https://www.ctp.go.cr/servicios/servicios-regulados/ruta-regular). The CTP also publishes press releases on temporary changes to stops and routes as PDFs, for example [Tope Nacional 2022 PDF](https://www.ctp.go.cr/Archivos/Publicaciones/121/Paradas%20y%20recorridos%20de%20buses%20cambian,%20de%20forma%20temporal,%20por%20el%20Tope%20Nacional%202022.pdf), and calls for permits on regular routes ([2024 notice](https://www.ctp.go.cr/Archivos/Publicaciones/185/CTP-DE-PI-COM-2024-032-CTP%20abre%20convocatoria%20pra%20permisos%20de%20operaci%C3%B3n%20en%20rutas%20regulares.pdf)).
- **Schedules:** ARESEP's FAQ says the CTP sets schedules, because it grants the concessions and permits. — [ARESEP FAQ](https://aresep.go.cr/participacion/preguntas-frecuentes-transporte/). A MINAE e-bus study got route and ramal georeferencing **from the CTP**, with GPS data supplied through RACSA, plus GIZ data. This shows the CTP holds route geometries that can be shared on request. — [MINAE technical report](https://minae.go.cr/energia/proyecto%20dando%20el%20salto%20a%20la%20movilidad%20ele%C2%B4ctrica/2.D4%20Technical%20report%20on%20bus%20line%20selection%20for%20pilot%20testing%20FINAL.pdf)
- **Operating schemes:** In November 2025 a decree let the CTP board fast-track operator studies that cut schedules and fleet on 27 routes. — [Semanario Universidad](https://semanariouniversidad.com/pais/ctp-usa-via-rapida-para-aprobar-estudios-de-las-autobuseras-para-reducir-horarios-y-flota-en-27-rutas/). ARESEP coordinates with the CTP on updating esquemas operativos (schedules and fleet per route). — [ARESEP news](https://aresep.go.cr/noticias/aresep-coordina-actualizacion-de-esquemas-operativos-rutas-bus-ctp/)
- **Data quality:** A CGR operational audit (January 2020 to June 2023) found a lack of information and little use of data for decisions. The CTP had **848 routes registered, 640 of them under some kind of permit**. — [Observador](https://observador.cr/servicio-de-buses-con-problemas-desde-las-paradas-hasta-los-permisos-contraloria-da-4-meses-al-mopt-para-presentar-solucion-integral/); audit PDF: [CGR](https://cgrfiles.cgr.go.cr/publico/docs_cgr/2023/SIGYD_D/SIGYD_D_2023022290.pdf). The Defensoría found the CTP lacks up-to-date, reliable data on abandoned routes. — same Observador article; [Semanario Universidad on CTP information gaps](https://semanariouniversidad.com/pais/defensoria-advierte-que-falta-de-informacion-en-ctp-podria-llevar-a-serias-falencias-en-renovacion-de-concesiones-de-bus/)
- The CTP acknowledged it has never measured demand route by route. Tariff-setting relies on operators' own data submitted to ARESEP's SIR. — [Semanario Universidad](https://semanariouniversidad.com/pais/pais-carece-de-estudios-confiables-sobre-demanda-de-servicio-de-bus-fijacion-de-tarifas-se-basa-en-datos-de-las-propias-empresas/)

### Inferences
- The CTP Visor is the best official source for **shapes.txt and stops.txt** (route paths, ramales, stops by direction). Check whether its exports are KML, SHP, CSV or GeoJSON. They are likely per-route or per-stop-query rather than a bulk dump, so expect to need scripted extraction or a formal data request.
- The CTP does not seem to publish schedules (horarios) as structured data. In practice, timetables come from operator websites, posted signs, ARESEP's 2020 PDFs (see section 2) or the Simovi/CTP GTFS programme (section 4).
- The "routes" count varies a lot by definition: 848 registered routes in the CTP registry (CGR), about 428 operating routes (CRHoy 2023), and thousands of tariff lines (ARESEP). A GTFS route_id scheme needs to settle whether a route is the CTP route code or the ramal.

### Gaps
- The URL and dataset list of the CTP open-data portal, and whether it has a bulk list of routes, operators, fleet and schedules. Could not be fetched.
- The exact export formats of the Visor, and whether it has an underlying ArcGIS or GeoServer REST endpoint that can be queried in bulk.
- No public CTP registry of fleet size per route was found.

## 2. ARESEP: per-route tariffs (pliego tarifario), route paths, passengers and schedules

### Takeaway
ARESEP is the **most machine-readable official source**. Its open-data section has (a) a **pliego tarifario web service**: a JSON/REST endpoint, exportable to CSV, listing every tariff by route, ramal and fraction (a fare segment within a ramal), with operators. It also has (b) a **WFS layer of GPS-surveyed bus ramales** (2015-2018), (c) passengers and revenue by route, and (d) historical tariffs. Tariffs change at least twice a year through extraordinary adjustments, plus ad hoc rulings.

### Cited Findings
- ARESEP open-data hub: [aresep.go.cr/datos-abiertos](https://aresep.go.cr/datos-abiertos/) (also [transparencia/datos-abiertos](https://aresep.go.cr/transparencia/datos-abiertos)). It lists bus route maps ("Mapas con el recorrido de las rutas de autobús") and bus fares classified by route, ramal and fraction. — [ARESEP datos abiertos](https://aresep.go.cr/datos-abiertos/)
- **Pliego tarifario web service:** ARESEP's site exports CSV from `https://datos.aresep.go.cr/ws.datosabiertos/Services/IT/PliegoTarifario.svc/ObtenerPliegoTarifarioAutobus/{YYYY-MM-DD}`. The date parameter returns the tariff schedule in force on that date. This was decoded from the base64 parameter of an ARESEP CSV export link, export name "_Tarifas_de_Autobús", example date 2024-06-10. — [ARESEP CSV export link](https://aresep.go.cr/exportar/csv.php?url=aHR0cHM6Ly9kYXRvcy5hcmVzZXAuZ28uY3Ivd3MuZGF0b3NhYmllcnRvcy9TZXJ2aWNlcy9JVC9QbGllZ29UYXJpZmFyaW8uc3ZjL09idGVuZXJQbGllZ29UYXJpZmFyaW9BdXRvYnVzLzIwMjQtMDYtMTA%3D&n=X1RhcmlmYXNfZGVfQXV0b2Jfcw%3D%3D). This endpoint was not reachable from this environment and needs a live test.
- Historical bus tariffs dataset: [ARESEP Tarifas históricas del servicio de autobús](https://aresep.go.cr/datos-abiertos/tarifas-autobus/). Current tariff lookup: https://aresep.go.cr/autobus/tarifas, plus a free ARESEP mobile app. — [ARESEP news](https://aresep.go.cr/noticias/aresep-aprueba-ajuste-tarifario-en-buses-y-taxis-por-alza-en-combustibles/); [La Nación on the ARESEP app](https://www.nacion.com/el-pais/app-de-aresep-permite-a-usuarios-ver-tarifas-de-bus-en-su-celular/FIBK2CARYJEGHEJIK3PNLPVDDI/story/)
- A 2023 report said there were **428 operating bus routes** and a pliego tarifario with **more than 4,000 tariffs**. — [CRHoy](https://crhoy.com/nacionales/aresep-avala-rebaja-en-tarifas-de-buses/)
- **Route-path dataset:** ARESEP surveyed bus route paths nationwide by GPS, identifying each ramal. WFS layer `PR05_Vista_BusRamales` is on ARESEP's map server under service `I_Transporte_Externo_PII`, in CRS CR05 (CRTM05). Published 2019-11-07, coverage 2015-2018, update frequency "annual", access listed under a licence restriction. A separate datos.aresep.go.cr endpoint exists for bus-stop map information. — [ARESEP Rutas de autobuses](https://aresep.go.cr/datos-abiertos/rutas-autobuses/) / [alt URL](https://aresep.go.cr/transparencia/datos-abiertos/rutas-autobuses)
- Passengers and revenue by bus route: [ARESEP Pasajeros movilizados e ingresos autobuses](https://aresep.go.cr/transparencia/datos-abiertos/pasajeros-movilizados-autobuses); see also [ARESEP transparenta datos del servicio de bus](https://aresep.go.cr/noticias/aresep-transparenta-datos-del-servicio-de-bus/).
- **Schedules (2020, dated):** During COVID, ARESEP posted 172 schedules from 43 operators online (Boletín 21-2020, 6 May 2020). By 18 May 2020 it had schedules for more than 300 bus routes available to download, plus train and ferry schedules. — [ARESEP coloca en línea horarios de buses](https://aresep.go.cr/noticias/aresep-coloca-linea-horarios-buses/); [ARESEP publica información en línea](https://aresep.go.cr/noticias/aresep-publica-informacion-linea-transporte-publico/)
- **Update frequency:** ARESEP reviews bus and taxi cost components every six months on its own initiative (salaries, fuel, maintenance, exchange rate, admin costs). — [Delfino (Feb 2026)](https://delfino.cr/2026/02/aresep-propone-ajustes-en-tarifas-de-buses-y-taxis). On 21 May 2026 it approved a general **5.43%** increase for routes with a designated operator and a valid licence. Examples: route 7 San José–Cementerio–Sabana went from ₡295 to ₡310, and route 427 Heredia–San Antonio de Belén from ₡335 to ₡355. — [ARESEP](https://aresep.go.cr/noticias/aresep-aprueba-ajuste-tarifario-en-buses-y-taxis-por-alza-en-combustibles/); [Delfino May 2026](https://delfino.cr/2026/05/aresep-aprueba-aumento-en-buses-y-taxis-por-alza-en-combustibles). An August 2026 proposal (public consultation closed 24 August) included fare cuts; its final approval was not confirmed. — [Delfino Aug 2026](https://delfino.cr/2026/08/aresep-propone-ajustes-en-tarifas-de-taxis-y-buses)
- ARESEP does not set fares for international routes; the CTP handles those. — search summary of [ARESEP FAQ](https://aresep.go.cr/participacion/preguntas-frecuentes-transporte/)
- In 2025, 67% of abandoned routes had no operator or valid operating scheme, so no tariff can be set for them. — [Delfino Oct 2025](https://delfino.cr/2025/10/95-de-las-rutas-de-buses-en-abandono-estan-en-la-zona-rural)
- ARESEP's archive holds route tariff dossiers from 1996 onward. These are historical only. — e.g. [archivodigital.go.cr](https://archivodigital.go.cr/index.php/expediente-que-contiene-estudio-tarifario-a-nivel-nacional-propuesto-por-el-ministerio-de-obras-publicas-y-transportes-para-el-transporte-publico-de-autobus-rutas-entre-san-jose-y-las-comunidades-de-heredia-la-aurora-de-heredia-por-el-)

### Inferences
- The pliego tarifario endpoint is probably the best **seed list for routes.txt, agency.txt and fare_attributes.txt**: a complete register of every route, ramal, fraction and operator that has a valid tariff. Fractions (stop-to-stop fare segments) map to GTFS fare_rules or Fares v2 with zones. Re-pull it after each ARESEP resolution, at least twice a year.
- The ARESEP WFS ramales layer (2015-2018) is a fallback for shapes, but it is 8 or more years old. Use the CTP Visor as the primary source and the ARESEP WFS for cross-checks.

### Gaps
- The exact JSON schema and fields of the pliego endpoint (route code, ramal description, operator name and ID, fraction, tariff, resolution number) were not inspected.
- Whether ARESEP's 2020 schedule PDFs are still online, and whether any current schedule dataset exists.
- Whether the WFS "licence restriction" forbids redistribution.

## 3. GIS layers (SNIT, IGN, MOPT, municipal, PRODUS-UCR, sectorización studies)

### Takeaway
No public SNIT or MOPT shapefile of bus routes or stops was found. The real official geometry sources are the CTP Visor (2026) and the ARESEP WFS (2015-2018). The sectorización plan for the GAM has been redesigned several times. The 2021 concession renewal made sector-based operation in the San José metropolitan area a goal over six years. The MOPT has since dropped the earlier plan and is redoing the studies to include the electric train, with a pilot targeted for 2027.

### Cited Findings
- A search for SNIT or MOPT geoportal bus route and stop layers returned nothing for Costa Rica. — search result set (no CR hits); see [OSM wiki CR public transport](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Costa_Rica/Public_transport), which mentions SITGAM and Proyecto Nodos only as proposed systems.
- Sectorización timeline: in 2023 the CTP defined five initial sectors (Tibás–Santo Domingo, Moravia–Coronado, Guadalupe–Coronado, Moravia–Paracito, Pavas, with San Francisco de Dos Ríos to follow) using business consortia. — [CRHoy](https://crhoy.com/nacionales/el-enorme-pendiente-de-la-sectorizacion-de-buses-se-saldara/); [CRHoy consorcios](https://www.crhoy.com/nacionales/gobierno-plantea-consorcios-empresariales-en-sectorizacion-de-rutas-de-bus/). The MOPT planned the first line (Tibás) for December 2024 and 12 lines by 2026; that did not happen. — [Observador](https://observador.cr/primera-linea-de-sectorizacion-del-transporte-publico-estara-habilitada-en-diciembre-preve-el-mopt/). The MOPT then dropped the previous plan and is updating studies to include the electric train with a German organisation, aiming for a 2027 pilot of "strategic routes" (article undated). — [Observador](https://observador.cr/mopt-desecha-anterior-plan-de-sectorizacion-del-transporte-publico-y-actualizara-estudios-para-incluir-el-tren-electrico/)
- The 2021 concession renewal covered 227 bus companies, 35 of them in the San José metropolitan area. It mandated a move from route-based to sector and subsector operation over six years in four stages. — [Delfino Sep 2021](https://delfino.cr/2021/09/ctp-renueva-concesiones-a-227-empresas-autobuseras-exigira-cambio-de-flotilla-cobro-electrico-y-sectorizacion)
- The OSM Spanish wiki describes the planned sectorised structure: **17 high-capacity trunk lines, 6 intersectoral routes and 15 secondary or internal routes**, with little information yet. The figure is about two years old. — [OSM wiki ES](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/ES:Costa_Rica/Transporte_p%C3%BAblico) (via search summary)
- An IDB technical-cooperation document (CR-T1119) is associated with GAM sectorization work. — [IADB CR-T1119 PDF](https://ewsdata.rightsindevelopment.org/files/documents/19/IADB-CR-T1119_tqFHUWa.pdf) (contents not reviewed)
- A CRUSA e-bus roadmap for the AMSJ (May 2025), citing 2022 CTP data, says the service is covered by **36 companies using 1,650 buses**. — [CRUSA Hoja de ruta autobuses eléctricos AMSJ](https://crusa.cr/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Hoja-de-ruta-autobuses-electricos-AMSJ.pdf) (via search summary). This PDF probably contains route-level analysis; worth reading.

### Inferences
- Do not plan around sectorización changing route structures before 2027 or later. Build against the current route-by-route system.
- Academic and consultancy studies (CRUSA, MINAE/GIZ, the IDB) probably hold route shapefiles obtained from the CTP. They are useful for contacts, not as public downloads.

### Gaps
- No SNIT (snitcr.go.cr) WMS or WFS transit layer was confirmed. No municipal (MSJ) geoportal bus layer was found. PRODUS-UCR and older LCR Logística or JICA shapefiles were not found online.

## 4. GTFS feeds and open-source projects (Mobility Database, Transitland, GitHub)

### Takeaway
**There is no official bus GTFS for the GAM yet, but one is being built.** In 2026 the CTP announced a national passenger-information system based on GTFS, built technically by UCR's **SIMOVI lab** (Fabián Abarca) through Fundación UCR, with MOPT, ARESEP and INCOFER. Separately, Fundación CPSU, with UK Embassy funding, ran a GTFS pilot for San José metro routes with SIMOVI and MobilityData. The only official Costa Rica GTFS in the Mobility Database is **INCOFER's train feed**, hosted by SIMOVI.

### Cited Findings
- **CTP national system (August 2026):** the CTP announced an official public platform to look up routes, stops, schedules, frequencies and fares, with real-time alerts added gradually, using GTFS. SIMOVI handles technical execution through Fundación UCR, drawing on CTP, ARESEP and INCOFER data. The Plan Estratégico Sectorial MOPT 2026-2030 makes the CTP responsible for adding routes and funds GTFS maintenance from its ordinary budget, with an estimated minimum of ₡15 million per year. — [Delfino Aug 2026](https://delfino.cr/2026/08/ctp-anuncia-creacion-de-sistema-nacional-con-informacion-de-rutas-horarios-y-tarifas-de-transporte-publico); governance caveats and EU and GIZ support are mentioned in [La República op-ed](https://www.larepublica.net/la-gobernanza-necesaria-detras-del-dato/)
- **CPSU pilot (March 2026):** Fundación Centro para la Sostenibilidad Urbana converted existing service information into GTFS for **pilot routes in the San José metropolitan area**, funded by the UK Embassy and supported by UCR's lab and MobilityData. — [Delfino Mar 2026](https://delfino.cr/2026/03/proyecto-busca-mejorar-la-informacion-del-transporte-publico-para-personas-usuarias-y-tomadores-de-decisiones)
- **INCOFER GTFS:** Mobility Database `mdb-3480`, producer URL `https://feeds.simovi.org/incofer/schedule/feed.zip`, 3 rail routes, validator showed 0 errors and 0 warnings, quality report updated August 2026. — [Mobility Database mdb-3480](https://mobilitydatabase.org/feeds/gtfs/mdb-3480)
- Mobility Database `mdb-2151` is a private **Daytrip Shuttle** feed (16 "bus" routes, tourist shuttles), not public transit. — [Mobility Database mdb-2151](https://mobilitydatabase.org/feeds/gtfs/mdb-2151)
- **SIMOVI lab GitHub** ([github.com/simovilab](https://github.com/simovilab), 61 repos; GitHub search, verified): `databus` (GTFS Schedule and Realtime server, Django/PostgreSQL/Redis, about 101 stars, active September 2026), `infobus` (passenger-information distribution using GTFS and GTFS-RT, updated 2026-10-06), `databus-editor` (GTFS editor with a map interface), `gtfs-django` / `django-app-gtfs`, `infobus-otp` (OpenTripPlanner container), `databus-app` (driver and fleet GPS telemetry app), `databus-stop-times` (arrival prediction), `infobus-web`/`-app`/`-screens`/`-mcp`, `reporte-sitp` (InfoTP design guide), `architecture` (updated 2026-10-09). The GitHub profile describes the lab as part of UCR's Escuela de Ingeniería Eléctrica and an academic member of MobilityData.
- Other GitHub repos (GitHub search "gtfs costa rica", 3 results, verified):
  - [tropicalizacion/buses](https://github.com/tropicalizacion/buses): UCR TC-691 "Tropicalización de la Tecnología" website with GTFS info for an operator (routes San Gabriel, Acosta); Django; updated June 2026.
  - [fabianabarca/editor-legacy](https://github.com/fabianabarca/editor-legacy): GTFS editing platform adapted for Costa Rica (2022-2023).
  - [DanixHerrera/transit-data-cr](https://github.com/DanixHerrera/transit-data-cr): created 2026-07-30, "compilation of official sources of CR public transport information: routes, stops, schedules and fares" (topics ctp, gtfs, kml, paradas, tarifas), with 25 open issues. The README is a stub; the contents could not be listed from here.
- [grote/osm2gtfs](https://github.com/grote/osm2gtfs) includes a Costa Rica config, but only for the suburban **train** (OSM geometry plus INCOFER schedules). — search summary of [OSM wiki CR public transport](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Costa_Rica/Public_transport) and [ecosyste.ms](https://ost.ecosyste.ms/projects/382)
- Mobility Database holds more than 6,000 feeds in more than 99 countries. — [MobilityDatabase about](https://mobilitydatabase.org/about)

### Inferences
- The most practical path is probably to **partner with or build on SIMOVI's stack and data**: databus, the editor, infobus and OTP. If the CTP/SIMOVI GTFS gets published, as the INCOFER feed was on feeds.simovi.org, it will become the canonical feed. A new app should plan to consume it rather than compete with it.
- Watch `feeds.simovi.org` and Mobility Database for new Costa Rica bus feeds.

### Gaps
- Transitland listings for Costa Rica could not be checked (transit.land was blocked; no search hits).
- Whether the CPSU pilot GTFS is publicly downloadable, and which routes it covers.
- A broader GitHub search ("rutas buses costa rica", code search for `agency.txt` with CR operators) was not possible: repository search returned only the three repos above for "gtfs costa rica".

## 5. OpenStreetMap: bus route relations, GAM coverage and Overpass

### Takeaway
OSM bus coverage in Costa Rica looks sparse and unevenly maintained. The "Bus routes in Costa Rica" wiki page is marked work-in-progress, with most listed Alajuela routes at 0/4 routing and 0/4 stops, and an empty San José table in the excerpt seen. There is an operator page for Grupo Tuasa (route 200 Alajuela–San José and variants). The number of route relations could not be counted because Overpass was blocked.

### Cited Findings
- The [Bus routes in Costa Rica](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Bus_routes_in_Costa_Rica) wiki page is marked "work in progress, likely incomplete". It uses a 0/4 to 4/4 completeness rating for routing and stops, lists routes by origin for San José and Alajuela, and most Alajuela entries are 0/4. — search summary
- [Costa Rica/Public transport](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Costa_Rica/Public_transport) says "more info to be added soon" for buses. Train route relations exist, and a GTFS is generated with osm2gtfs from OSM geometry plus INCOFER schedules. The Transportr app shows INCOFER itineraries. — search summary
- [OSM wiki: Grupo Tuasa](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Grupo_Tuasa) covers route 200 Alajuela–San José via INVU Cañas, plus variants via Radial and Heredia. It points to https://www.grupotuasa.com/modulos/rutas.html as official info that "can be outdated". Some variants have unknown codes. — search summary
- The Costa Rican OSM community (Jaime) helped develop osm2gtfs in 2017-2018, from the MapaNica, Nicaragua effort. — [OSM diary](https://api.openstreetmap.org/user/pantierra/diary/44538); [HOT list 2017](https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/hot/2017-January/012866.html)

### Inferences
- These Overpass queries are untested; run them on overpass-turbo.eu:
  - Count Costa Rica bus route relations: `[out:json][timeout:120]; area["ISO3166-1"="CR"][admin_level=2]->.cr; rel(area.cr)["type"="route"]["route"="bus"]; out count;`
  - Export GAM routes with members (bbox roughly 9.80,-84.35,10.10,-83.85): `[out:json][timeout:180]; rel["type"="route"]["route"="bus"](9.80,-84.35,10.10,-83.85); out body; >; out skel qt;`
  - Bus stops: `node["highway"="bus_stop"](area.cr); out count;` and `node["public_transport"="platform"](area.cr); out count;`
  - Route masters and operators: `rel["type"="route_master"]["route_master"="bus"](area.cr); out tags;`, then group by `operator=*` and `ref=*`.
- OSM is useful as the street network for routing (OTP and map matching), but it should not be the primary source of route definitions in the GAM. CTP Visor exports could be map-matched and contributed back, subject to licence compatibility with ODbL.

### Gaps
- An actual count of route relations and stops in Costa Rica and the GAM, and their last-edit dates. Overpass and OSM hosts were blocked.

## 6. Unofficial compilations and consumer apps

### Takeaway
No complete unofficial directory was found. Moovit has GAM line listings (with schedules) of variable accuracy. ICE's kölbi+ app claims schedules for more than 4,000 bus routes. Canatrans (the operators' chamber) has announced a real-time stops and buses app. Operator websites and Facebook pages remain the main source of timetables.

### Cited Findings
- Moovit mirrors its line pages and PDF line maps, for example San José–Alajuelita–Concepción (weekdays about 5:00 to 20:00) and San José–Naranjo. — [Moovit asset PDF](https://appassets.mvtdev.com/map/188/l/2967/48315607.pdf). Users report Moovit is slightly inaccurate on local arrival times (2023). — [Drexel study-abroad blog](https://studyabroadblog.drexel.edu/2023/03/13/transportation-and-traveling-from-san-jose/)
- ICE's **kölbi+** iOS app "includes schedules of more than 4,000 bus routes". — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id610059339) (via search summary; unverified, and the figure probably counts ramales and schedules)
- Canatrans plans an app with real-time locations of stops and buses (no launch date). — [Observador](https://observador.cr/autobuseros-desarrollaran-app-con-ubicacion-de-paradas-y-buses-en-tiempo-real/)
- Tourist-oriented intercity compilations: [Visit Costa Rica (ICT) "Itinerario de buses" PDF, August 2024](https://www.visitcostarica.com/sites/default/files/2024-08/Itinerario%20de%20buses.pdf) (lists terminals and operators such as Tracopa, Tralapa, Tuasa, Station Wagon, Musoc, Coopetrans), [Geckoroutes bus guide](https://www.geckoroutes.com/costa-rica/costa-rica-bus-and-minivan-guide/), [costa-rica-guide.com bus routes](https://costa-rica-guide.com/travel/transportation/costa-rica-bus-routes/).
- UCR publishes PDFs with external bus schedules and fares for routes serving the campus (2022, 2024). — [UCR 2024 PDF](https://www.ucr.ac.cr/medios/documentos/2024/horarios-y-tarifas-buses-externos-i-ciclo-2024-b-65fde4113d760.pdf); [UCR bus externo](https://www.ucr.ac.cr/acerca-u/campus/bus-externo.html)
- A list of Costa Rica open APIs exists: [ruiznorlan/public-apis-cr](https://github.com/ruiznorlan/public-apis-cr) (contents not reviewed).

### Inferences
- Moovit and kölbi+ data are proprietary and cannot be reused for a GTFS. Use them only to validate.

### Gaps
- No Wikipedia list of San José bus routes or terminals was confirmed. Facebook groups and pages of operators were not surveyed, because searches returned nothing useful.

## 7. Scale of the system (routes, operators, buses, riders) and main terminals

### Takeaway
Figures conflict because of definitions and dates. Nationally: 848 routes registered with the CTP (CGR, as of 2023), about 428 operating routes (2023), and more than 4,000 tariff lines. ARESEP has counted 355 service providers (2021), but by 2025 only 182 operators had a valid tariff record. Ridership was about 1.6 million passengers per day nationally (2023) and has fallen 42% since 2018. In the GAM: about 35-36 companies and about 1,650 buses (2021-2022 CTP data), with roughly 3,000 GAM buses expected to have electronic payment.

### Cited Findings
- **Routes:** 848 registered with the CTP, 640 of them under a permit (CGR audit 2020-2023). — [Observador](https://observador.cr/servicio-de-buses-con-problemas-desde-las-paradas-hasta-los-permisos-contraloria-da-4-meses-al-mopt-para-presentar-solucion-integral/). 428 operating routes and more than 4,000 tariffs (2023). — [CRHoy](https://crhoy.com/nacionales/aresep-avala-rebaja-en-tarifas-de-buses/). About 109 lines abandoned by end-2025 (Canatrans); the CTP says more than 107 routes were returned or abandoned. — [Semanario Universidad](https://semanariouniversidad.com/?p=375000). Abandoned routes are concentrated in rural areas (95%). — [Delfino Oct 2025](https://delfino.cr/2025/10/95-de-las-rutas-de-buses-en-abandono-estan-en-la-zona-rural). Recent returns include Barrio Luján–San José. — [Observador](https://observador.cr/dos-rutas-de-autobus-han-sido-devueltas-al-ctp-en-15-dias-canatrans-alerta-por-proliferacion-de-servicios-ilegales/)
- **Operators:** ARESEP registered 355 bus service providers. Ten groups receive half of the sector's revenue, about 30 groups hold more than 3 concessions or permits, and 4 groups hold 10 or more. The business is worth about US$385M per year (2021). — [Semanario Universidad, "¿Quiénes son los dueños de los autobuses?"](https://semanariouniversidad.com/especiales/quienes-son-los-duenos-de-los-autobuses-en-costa-rica/). Estado de la Nación 2025, citing ARESEP: operators fell from 240 to 182 (-24.2%), and concessionaire revenue fell from ₡19,452M (2018) to ₡13,833M (2025). The period of the operator decline is ambiguous in the sources. — [Semanario Universidad on Estado de la Nación 2025](https://semanariouniversidad.com/?p=375000). The 2021 renewal covered 227 companies, 35 in the AMSJ. — [Delfino 2021](https://delfino.cr/2021/09/ctp-renueva-concesiones-a-227-empresas-autobuseras-exigira-cambio-de-flotilla-cobro-electrico-y-sectorizacion)
- **Buses:** AMSJ has 36 companies and 1,650 buses (CTP 2022 data, cited by CRUSA 2025). — [CRUSA PDF](https://crusa.cr/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Hoja-de-ruta-autobuses-electricos-AMSJ.pdf). SINPE-TP electronic payment: 20 GAM companies with 1,151 buses at one point, with a target of about 3,000 GAM buses equipped by end-2025. — [Revista Summa](https://revistasumma.com/?p=227855). A later report said 50 operators with 1,725 buses on the system in the GAM (date unclear). — [Semanario Universidad](https://semanariouniversidad.com/pais/usuarios-de-140-buses-de-san-jose-alajuela-y-heredia-podran-utilizar-el-sistema-de-pago-electronico/). *Conflict:* 1,650 buses (2022 CTP) against "about 3,000 GAM buses" (Summa). The counts may differ by scope (AMSJ against the wider GAM).
- **Riders:** about 1.6 million bus passengers per day nationally (2023). — [CRHoy](https://crhoy.com/nacionales/aresep-avala-rebaja-en-tarifas-de-buses/). Public-transport passengers fell 42.2% between 2018 and 2025, while cars grew 62.1% between 2013 and 2025 (Estado de la Nación 2025). — [Semanario Universidad](https://semanariouniversidad.com/?p=375000); [La Nación](https://www.nacion.com/el-pais/transporte-publico-en-lenta-agonia-estas-son-las/RB2TU6JU7ZBUJHIEBXURKC6CQY/story/). ARESEP reports a 36% drop in users, with some routes down 100%. — [Observador](https://observador.cr/aresep-teme-que-mas-rutas-de-buses-sean-abandonadas-ante-menor-numero-de-usuarios/)
- **Electric buses:** BIUSA (Grupo Tracopa) put the first 25 electric buses into regular service (King Long) on San José–La Carpio–León XIII. — [Observador](https://observador.cr/fotos-25-buses-electricos-cubriran-las-rutas-de-san-jose-a-la-carpio-y-leon-xiii/). An earlier 3-bus pilot ran with ATD (San José–Desamparados), Transplusa (San José–La Unión) and TUASA (San José–Alajuela). — [Delfino 2021](https://delfino.cr/2021/02/ruta-san-jose-desamparados-cuenta-con-sus-primeros-buses-electricos)
- **Terminals:** Terminal 7-10 closed as a transport hub on 30 April 2025, displacing at least 7 companies. Routes moved to Terminal Atlántico Norte (Ciudad Quesada/San Carlos, Monteverde, Jacó, Nica Expreso), Transportes Inteligentes on Paseo Colón (Cóbano, Nicoya, Santa Cruz), and Comtrasuli near the Coca-Cola area (Nicabus, Transportes del Sol). — [El Financiero](https://www.elfinancierocr.com/negocios/terminal-de-buses-7-10-cierra-sus-puertas-esto-es/A34JDERDMJH33CHEGTUDT6CKYU/story/); [Teletica May 2025](https://www.teletica.com/nacional/tras-cierre-de-terminal-de-buses-7-10-a-donde-deben-ir-los-pasajeros_384023). Other San José terminals named: Terminal Coca-Cola (for example Coopetrans to Atenas, Calle 16 between Avenidas 1 and 3) and Gran Terminal del Caribe / Los Caribeños, plus operator terminals (Tracopa, Tralapa, Tuasa, Station Wagon, Musoc). — [ICT bus itinerary PDF 2024](https://www.visitcostarica.com/sites/default/files/2024-08/Itinerario%20de%20buses.pdf); [Geckoroutes](https://www.geckoroutes.com/costa-rica/costa-rica-bus-and-minivan-guide/)

### Inferences
- For a GAM app, plan for about 35-50 operators and roughly 300-400 route codes with several ramales each. That is an estimate derived from the figures above, not a sourced number.
- Data volatility is high: routes are being returned or abandoned, schedules are being cut (27 routes in November 2025), and tariffs are adjusted at least twice a year. A GTFS pipeline needs a scheduled refresh against the ARESEP pliego and CTP Visor.

### Gaps
- No authoritative current (2026) count of GAM routes, GAM buses or GAM daily riders was found. The Estado de la Nación 2025 transport chapter was not read directly.
